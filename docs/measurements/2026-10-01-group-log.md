# The range replica on hyper-log's group handle

**Question.** mantle moved onto hyper-log at hyper-raft `50a711d`, where every read of the log
was a round trip to its owner thread and a committed entry of a three-member group took
0.83 ms against `crates/log`'s 0.16 ms (measurements/2026-10-01-shared-log.md). hyper-raft
`8fe3f23` adds a group's handle, `GroupLog`, that answers the core's reads on the replica's
thread from what the replica's own answered writes left, and lets a blocking writer do its
own frame's write and flush (hyper-log `ORIGIN.md`). The range store now holds the group's
handle and keeps nothing of the group's state itself (`crates/range/src/store.rs`). Does the
range replay the same, and do wall time, allocations and reallocations come to `crates/log`'s
or below?

**Machine.** macOS 26.4.1 on an Apple M5 Max (18 cores, 128 GiB), APFS on the internal SSD,
`F_FULLFSYNC`. Other sessions built and tested on the machine throughout; the load average
is given with every run, read from `vm.loadavg` before each.

**Builds**, all release for timing, debug for the simulation:
- *group-log*: this change, `shared-log` with hyper-raft `8fe3f23` snapshots;
- *a2021df*: `ci-six` at `a2021df`, `crates/log` under the same Raft core (hyper-raft
  `dce1daa`), the build the move to hyper-log was measured against;
- *3b16867*: mantle `dev` at `3b16867`, `crates/log` under the Raft core before R-2;
- *03bda33*: `shared-log` before this change, hyper-log `50a711d`, for reference.

## The simulation replays the same

`crates/range/tests/sim.rs` was run for seeds 1 to 48 on this change and on `a2021df` with a
temporary hook, not kept, that appends each seed's number and its `Ran` (its counters and
every gateway operation, key, input, output, and the steps it was called and answered at) to
a file:

```
MANTLE_SIM_SEEDS=48 MANTLE_SIM_RECORD=<file> cargo test -p mantle-range --test sim a_group_under_faults
```

The two files are byte-identical, MD5 `5583f0469593de8cd73c982d52789e49`, the same digest the
move to hyper-log recorded for `crates/log`. The 48 seeds took 4.21 s on this change and
4.61 s on `a2021df`, run side by side at a load average of 31–32.

The core reads its storage only between the member's own writes, and only those writes move
what it reads; the handle applies each answered write as the log did. A unit test checks
the store's bounds, terms and entries against the log's own after writes that append,
conflict and truncate, with no read sent to the log's owner (`GroupLog::asked` stays 0), and
that the log refuses the group's writes from anyone but the handle (`crates/range/src/store.rs`,
`the_cores_reads_are_answered_by_the_groups_handle_as_the_log_answers_them`).

## The replica's path

The harness of the earlier measurement, again as a temporary test not kept: a group of three
replicas on simulated devices in one process, the first elected, then driven to commit one
registration at a time, each followed by delivering messages and driving every member until
none is left (`settle` of `crates/range/tests/group.rs`, `drive` waiting on each write), 50
entries to warm and 300 measured. A counting global allocator counts every allocation and
reallocation in the process, the logs' threads included. The same source ran on each build,
its imports and simulated file type aside. Each round ran every build once, the order rotated
from round to round.

Per committed entry:

| | group-log | a2021df | 3b16867 | 03bda33 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| allocations | 127.2 | 262.2 | 271.2 | 127.2 |
| reallocations | 4.0 | 25.0 | 25.0 | 4.0 |
| bytes allocated | 145,582 | 282,345 | 291,027 | 145,367 |
| wall, 10 rounds at load 7.8: median (range) | 74.2 µs (49.3–86.6) | 75.6 µs (51.6–78.3) | 75.4 µs (50.6–81.8) | 299 µs (225–342) |
| wall, 31 rounds at load 32.3–32.6: median (range) | 117 µs (62–326) | 133 µs (69–418) | 124 µs (72–341) | 530 µs (205–1256) |

The counts were the same in every run. Paired by round, group-log was faster than `a2021df` in
7 of 10 and 25 of 31 rounds (median ratio 0.97 and 0.95), than `3b16867` in 7 of 10 and 24 of
31 (0.96, 0.93), and than `03bda33` in every round (0.25, 0.23). The handle keeps 215 bytes an
entry more than `03bda33` allocated: its runs of terms and its copy of the recent entries'
bytes, which the log no longer keeps for a claimed group.

With one replica every build runs one append a flush, and they are the same. At 256 appends of
16 KiB group-log ran more than `a2021df` in 13 of 28 paired rounds, within 4% either way. At
256 appends of 128 B it ran fewer, in 24 of 28 rounds against `a2021df` and 19 of 28 against
`3b16867`, by 2–4% at the medians. `a2021df` and `3b16867` run the same `crates/log` and the
same benchmark, its output's idle column aside, so the spread between them, up to 4% at that
point, is the runs' own.

The cause is in how many appends a flush carries. At 256 appends of 128 B, group-log's
flushes carried 249–254 appends each in every run, `a2021df`'s 127–251 and `3b16867`'s
127–253: hyper-log gathers nearly every replica into each frame, and so flushes about 105
times a second where `crates/log` flushed about 150, each of its appends waiting for the
replicas behind it. At that point its p99 was lower in every batch (12.95 ms against 14.2 at the last).
This is hyper-log's writer and the wait it anticipates its returning submitters for, which
mantle takes as vendored (vendor/UPSTREAM.md); the range store's change does not touch the
path `bench log` drives.

The process ran one thread more at every point than `crates/log`, as before: the log's owner
and its I/O thread.

## The chunk store's read buffers

Not a timing: 03bda33 reached one read-buffer pool from every reader of a volume through a
lock, and its tests reached one simulated file from the volume and the issuer's workers through
another. Each reader now owns its buffers (`ReadBuffers`; the writer, cleaner, scrubber and
recovery their own), and a test's simulated device is a thread of its own that its handles
send operations to (`crates/chunk/tests/common/device.rs`). The issuer test counts that thread
among the idle ones: 10 threads idle and 10 while writing, at most 32 writes between flushes
and 4 in flight at depth 4.
