# The range replica on hyper-log's group handle

**Question.** mantle moved onto hyper-log at hyper-raft `50a711d`, where every read of the log
was a round trip to its owner thread and a committed entry of a three-member group took
0.83 ms against `crates/log`'s 0.16 ms (measurements/2026-10-01-shared-log.md). hyper-raft
`8fe3f23` adds a group's handle, `GroupLog`, that answers the core's reads on the replica's
thread from what the replica's own answered writes left, and lets a blocking writer do its
own frame's write and flush; `f6f34eb` lets the handle cut an update into frames
(`GroupLog::parts`); and `63cb65d` sends a waking submission without waiting for its
admission (hyper-log `ORIGIN.md`). The range store holds only its group's handle and keeps
nothing of the group's state itself (`crates/range/src/store.rs`). Does the range replay the
same, and do wall time, allocations and reallocations come to `crates/log`'s or below?

**Machine.** macOS 26.4.1 on an Apple M5 Max (18 cores, 128 GiB), APFS on the internal SSD,
`F_FULLFSYNC`. Other sessions built and tested on the machine throughout; the load average
is given with every run, read from `vm.loadavg` before each.

**Builds**, all release for timing, debug for the simulation:
- *group-log*: this change, `shared-log` with hyper-raft `63cb65d` snapshots;
- *a2021df*: `ci-six` at `a2021df`, `crates/log` under the same Raft core (hyper-raft
  `dce1daa`), the build the move to hyper-log was measured against;
- *3b16867*: mantle `dev` at `3b16867`, `crates/log` under the Raft core before R-2. Its
  `crates/log` and `mantle bench log` are `a2021df`'s, the benchmark's idle column aside.

## The simulation replays the same

`crates/range/tests/sim.rs` was run for seeds 1 to 48 with a temporary hook, not kept, that
appends each seed's number and its `Ran` (its counters and every gateway operation, key,
input, output, and the steps it was called and answered at) to a file:

```
MANTLE_SIM_SEEDS=48 MANTLE_SIM_RECORD=<file> cargo test -p mantle-range --test sim a_group_under_faults
```

On this change the file's MD5 is `5583f0469593de8cd73c982d52789e49`, the digest the same hook
gave on `a2021df` the same day and the move to hyper-log recorded for `crates/log`: the two
are byte-identical. The 48 seeds took 4.18 s at a load average of 28.

The core reads its storage only between the member's own writes, and only those writes move
what it reads; the handle applies each answered write as the log did. A unit test checks
the store's bounds, terms and entries against the log's own after writes that append,
conflict and truncate, with no read sent to the log's owner (`GroupLog::asked` stays 0), and
that the log refuses the group's writes from anyone but the handle (`crates/range/src/store.rs`,
`the_cores_reads_are_answered_by_the_groups_handle_as_the_log_answers_them`).

## The replica's path

The harness of the earlier measurement, again as a temporary test not kept: a group of three
replicas on simulated devices in one process, the first elected, then driven to commit one
registration at a time, each followed by delivering messages and driving every member until
none is left (`settle` of `crates/range/tests/group.rs`, `drive` waiting on each write), 50
entries to warm and 300 measured. A counting global allocator counts every allocation and
reallocation in the process, the logs' threads included. The same source ran on each build,
its imports, its simulated file type and how it holds the logs aside. 31 rounds, each running
every build once, the order rotated from round to round, at a load average of 34.0–34.9.

Per committed entry:

| | group-log | a2021df | 3b16867 |
|---|---|---|---|
| allocations | 127.2 | 262.2 | 271.2 |
| reallocations | 4.0 | 25.0 | 25.0 |
| bytes allocated | 145,582 | 282,343–282,345 | 291,026–291,028 |
| wall: median (range) | 143 µs (135–348) | 151 µs (143–429) | 151 µs (147–432) |

The counts were the same in every run. Paired by round, group-log was faster than `a2021df` in
27 of 31 rounds (median ratio 0.96) and than `3b16867` in 24 of 31 (0.94). The handle keeps
215 bytes an entry more than hyper-log `50a711d` allocated: its runs of terms and its copy of
the recent entries' bytes, which the log no longer keeps for a claimed group.

At hyper-raft `8fe3f23`, before `GroupLog::parts`, the same harness gave the same counts and
117 µs against 133 µs and 124 µs (31 rounds, load 32.3–32.6), and 74 µs against 76 and 75
(10 rounds, load 7.8); hyper-log `50a711d` took 299–530 µs in those runs.

## `mantle bench log`

```
mantle bench log <dir> --skip-device --sizes 128,16384 --replicas 1,256
```

Each round ran every build once, the order rotated. Median appends a second (range) and median
p50 latency; the last column is the rounds in which group-log ran more appends a second than
each other build, paired by round, ties not counted.

Eight rounds, load 23.8–35.2:

| Point | group-log | a2021df | 3b16867 | more than a2021df, 3b16867 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 B, 1 | 121 (118–124), 8.65 ms | 120 (118–126), 8.65 ms | 119 (49–124), 8.65 ms | 4/8, 6/8 |
| 128 B, 256 | 28.7K (10.3K–29.6K), 8.65 ms | 27.5K (27.2K–28.1K), 9.70 ms | 27.3K (26.8K–27.8K), 9.70 ms | 6/8, 6/8 |
| 16 KiB, 1 | 122 (118–124), 8.65 ms | 122 (117–123), 8.65 ms | 121 (59–124), 8.65 ms | 5/8, 3/8 |
| 16 KiB, 256 | 23.2K (8.4K–23.7K), 11.0 ms | 21.4K (20.1K–21.8K), 12.1 ms | 21.7K (20.8K–22.4K), 11.8 ms | 7/8, 7/8 |

Twelve rounds of the 256-replica points alone (`--replicas 256`), load 5.3–24.5:

| Point | group-log | a2021df | 3b16867 | more than a2021df, 3b16867 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 128 B, 256 | 29.3K (26.2K–29.9K), 8.65 ms | 27.1K (16.2K–29.0K), 9.57 ms | 27.4K (11.9K–28.6K), 9.70 ms | 12/12, 12/12 |
| 16 KiB, 256 | 23.1K (20.5K–24.0K), 11.0 ms | 21.1K (19.3K–22.9K), 12.1 ms | 21.6K (16.5K–23.6K), 11.8 ms | 11/12, 10/12 |

With one replica every build runs one append a flush, and they are the same. At 256 replicas
group-log ran more appends a second than both `crates/log` builds at both sizes, and its p50 is
one flush (8.65 ms at 128 B) where theirs is 9.6–9.7 ms. Its flushes carry 250–254 appends at
128 B, theirs 141–251.

At hyper-raft `8fe3f23` the 128 B × 256 point ran 2–4% fewer appends a second than
`crates/log` (more than `a2021df` in 4 of 28 rounds), with the same 249–254 appends a flush.
The cause was not the frame's size: `Log::submit_waking` waited for the owner to admit each
submission, so a driver keeping many replicas' appends out sent its next only once the owner
had admitted the last, and the submitters a frame answered came back one owner wake at a time
while the device sat idle. Since `63cb65d` the call returns once the submission is sent, and
a refusal at admission arrives as the submission's answer, through its waker
(`crates/mantle/src/bench_log.rs` takes it as any other failed answer).

The process ran one thread more at every point than `crates/log`: the log's owner and its I/O
thread where `crates/log` ran one writer.

## The chunk store's read buffers

Not a timing: 03bda33 reached one read-buffer pool from every reader of a volume through a
lock, and its tests reached one simulated file from the volume and the issuer's workers through
another. Each reader now owns its buffers (`ReadBuffers`; the writer, cleaner, scrubber and
recovery their own), and a test's simulated device is a thread of its own that its handles
send operations to (`crates/chunk/tests/common/device.rs`). The issuer test counts that thread
among the idle ones: 10 threads idle and 10 while writing, at most 32 writes between flushes
and 4 in flight at depth 4.
