# The S3 listener's own cost on loopback

**Question.** What does the S3 listener (`crates/node/src/listener`, node.md §4.1–§4.5) cost as a
stock client sees it, itself and not the node behind it: a request's round trip, and an object's
bytes moved, over plain HTTP and over TLS 1.3? The baseline later work on the listener is judged
against.

**Method.** `mantle bench s3` (`crates/mantle/src/bench_s3.rs`), a release build, each row run for
two seconds, three rounds. The listener runs on one hyper-rt shard, as a node runs it, with the
node's object host played by a stub on the door's own interface (`crate::door`): a get's bytes
from one object in memory, handed out in the listener's 65,527-octet pieces, and a put's body
taken and dropped. So a row measures the codec, SigV4, the handover through the door, the response,
the socket and TLS, and the client; never the drivers, the ranges or the volumes. The client is a
thread of its own on one keep-alive connection to `127.0.0.1`, a request at a time, each head
signed once a row and sent again (SigV4 holds a signature fifteen minutes), so the client's signing
is no part of a round trip. A request's row is round trips: their count, median and 99th
percentile by nearest rank, and requests a second over the row. An object's row moves the AWS
CLI's part, 8 MiB (its default `multipart_chunksize`), again and again: bytes a second over the
row, a put's body plain (`UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD`) or `aws-chunked` with a trailing CRC-32 in botocore's
1 MiB chunks, as boto3 sends one. TLS is 1.3 with the endpoint's P-256 chain as `mantle init`
makes it.

**Measured.** This Mac: an Apple M5 Max (18 cores, 128 GiB), 2026-10-25 18:13, the release binary
built from the step's tree (md5 `f595ec7d67bf643072ba807ad975bb75`), other sessions' work running:
load averages 16.3, 24.3 and 24.6 at the first round. The median of three rounds:

**What it shows.** A request's round trip through the listener is 26 to 58 µs on loopback, plain or
under TLS: its parsing, routing, SigV4 check, handover to the host's task and back through the
door's channels, and its response, together; this run does not part them, and one row's round
trips doubled between rounds as other sessions' load moved. A part of 8 MiB moves at
5 to 6.7 GB/s plain on one connection; under TLS, its records sealed at one end and opened at the
other on one core each, at 1.3 to 2.1 GB/s, a quarter to a third of it. `aws-chunked` costs a put
no more than a plain body: its decoding is one copy of each octet the plain body makes too.

**Under heavier load.** An earlier run, of a 64 MiB object (the release binary md5
`b3a7c2df1717650d03b8b4513b6fb16c`, the same listener), at load averages near 30 the same evening
moved 1.5 to 2.3 GB/s plain while each object's median took 9.5 to 15 ms, 4 to 7 GB/s: a minority of
transfers took 150 to 300 ms (their p99s), so that run's rate was its stalls'. Where they come from,
the shard descheduled among other sessions' work or a wait inside the listener's exchange with the
host, is not yet traced.
