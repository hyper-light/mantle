# A cell of three in localkind's kind cluster: the scale step's tests through Argo CD

2026-11-04 and 2026-11-05, plan step 12 of the data layer (docs/design/kubernetes.md; research/78);
run 4 on the readiness lock (main `72511e15`), 2026-11-05.

**Question.** Plan step 12: does a cell of three that localkind's pipeline publishes and Argo CD
deploys into this Mac's Docker Desktop kind cluster pass the scale step's transfer and replication
tests: the 1k, 10k and 100k small sets through both doors, large objects as many as this Mac's free
space less its reserve holds after redundancy, each founder's pod killed in turn by Chaos Mesh with
every acknowledged object read back, and this Mac's free space never below its reserve?

**The machine and the platform.** This Mac (Apple M5 Max, 128 GiB, APFS on the internal SSD),
Docker Desktop 4.66.1 with its VM at 18 CPUs and 94 GiB, and its kind cluster: a control plane and
two workers, Kubernetes 1.36.4, arm64. localkind's platform was brought up again on it (localkind
`9189896` and later; it had been absent since 2026-10-22): Concourse, Argo CD, metrics-server,
Headlamp, Istio, Kiali, Grafana and Chaos Mesh 2.8.4, with only mantle's pipeline set, since the
other projects' pipelines would build and cache on this nearly full Mac beside the run. Docker
Desktop and each long step ran under launchd after Docker Desktop twice stopped as a session's turn
ended, where kiln ends what a turn's commands leave running.

**The pipeline and the deployment.** Concourse's `mantle` pipeline (localkind
`pipelines/mantle.yml`) built main `49aa075c`: the suites in 29 m 49 s (`test/3`; the workspace's
and the vendored crates', the stock S3 clients installed as mantle's CI installs them), lint in
44 s (`lint/2`: fmt, clippy `-D warnings`, the contract and production checks), and published the
image `lundheaudio/mantle:0.1.0-ci.872` and the chart `mantle-chart` 0.1.0-ci.872 pinned to it
(`publish/2`, 2 m 23 s). Argo CD's `mantle` app (localkind `argocd/apps/mantle.yaml`, out of the
ambient mesh) synced the newest chart; `scripts/apps/mantle-init.sh` made the cell once with the
published image's own `mantle init`, each founder reached at its Service's name, and set its
Secrets. The founders tolerate the control plane's taint, since the cluster has two workers and
each founder needs a Kubernetes node of its own: they ran on `desktop-control-plane`,
`desktop-worker` and `desktop-worker2`, each at 4 CPUs and 4 GiB, requests equal to limits. Argo CD
showed the app Synced and Healthy at 0.1.0-ci.872 for each run.

**The run.** `scripts/apps/mantle-scale.sh` ran mantle's transfer harness from a Job in the
cluster with `--cell` (`mantle bench transfer`, crates/mantle/src/transfer/outside.rs), its image
the published binary beside boto3, the AWS CLI v2 and kubectl, loaded into each node's containerd.
Halfway through the first small set's native puts, its disruption killed each founder's pod in turn
with a Chaos Mesh PodChaos `pod-kill`, waiting for each successor, a pod of a new UID, to be ready
(enlisted in its run) before the next. The volumes were stated at `mantle init`; a guard sampling
this Mac's free space every 30 s would have stopped the run below its 30 GiB reserve, and never did;
the samples read beside it ran from 64.6 GiB at the second run's start to 50.5 GiB at the third's
end.

**Run 1** (seed 1, 16.7 GB volumes) ended in its disruption: one native put found no node to take
it in the client's three attempts, which ask each node in turn, while the pods were being killed
(`ClientError::Untaken`, `NotConnected`), and the harness ended the run on it. A put no node took,
or whose answer was lost, while a disruption has the cell short a node is now counted as lost and
acknowledged by nothing, as a `SlowDown` refusal is (main `49aa075c`); any other failure still ends
the run. Its cell, claims and Secrets were removed and the cell made again.

**Why a founder its peers dial stayed away.** mantle-1's successor bound its ports within 2 s of its
kill, and its links to both peers stayed unkeyed: no range knew a leader, and its enlist was asked
and not answered. Its peers' links to it said 20 and 38 dials, each ended, the peer suspect. Both
peers' nodes' conntrack tables (`conntrack -L` from a privileged pod on the node's network, read in
the minute before 02:15:51 UTC) held the flow from the peer's port 7000 to mantle-1's Service's
cluster IP translated to the killed pod's address, `10.244.1.58`, where the successor was
`10.244.1.60`, assured and renewed by every dial. The entry on mantle-2's node was deleted by hand
at 02:15:51 (`conntrack -D`); mantle-1 was ready 4 s later, and both peers' links said keyed by
02:15:59. mantle-0's node's entry, untouched, had been replaced by then: kube-proxy's sync on
another Service's change, mantle-2's pod killed at 02:15:56, would remove it (research/78 [16]), but
what did was not traced. The other two successors dial their peers from their new pods, through new
flows, and were ready in 7 s. This run's disruption is therefore a traced one, shortened by the
entry deleted by hand.

**The refusals' causes.** Run 2's ten minutes of refusals "with every founder ready" had two causes,
both now measured. Its readiness was the record of the run last enlisted, which a killed founder's
directory keeps until its successor raises the run after recovering its stores: on this Mac's
loopback the check said a founder ready 57 ms after its `SIGKILL`, so Kubernetes counted pods ready
that were not serving, and `kill-each` took the next founder while the last was away. That is fixed
(main `72511e15`; `status_is_ready_only_while_a_living_process_of_the_node_is_enlisted`, which fails
on main `7d628026`). And a founder its peers dial is out of reach through its Service's cluster IP
until kube-proxy removes the flow's stale translation, which their dials keep alive; that is open,
its fix proposed in research/78 ("Open"). It is availability, not correctness: in every run every
acknowledged object read back exactly through every node.
