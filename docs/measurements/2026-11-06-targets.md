# The engine against RocksDB and SplinterDB, target by target

2026-11-05 and 2026-11-06, mantle `e118c09d` (a docs commit over `bc7354bf`, the commit whose CI is
green on every target). Release builds, unmodified unless a section says otherwise.

**Question.** On each supported target, does the engine hold 4-5x over RocksDB and SplinterDB on
fills, gets and seeks at 10M and 100M keys?

**Answer.** Not on every axis. 10M holds over RocksDB on every target measured, but for fills on
Linux x86_64 against the 16 GiB arm. 100M gets hold only on a machine with room for the kept
memtables. Over SplinterDB, fills and gets are below 4x at both sizes, as research/66 §6 already
recorded on the VM lane. Under `sample` at 100M on macOS, four runs gave wrong answers or corruption
errors, cause not found (below). Windows was not measured. Metadata replication (step 3) was not
measured in this run.

## How it ran

The owner's harness (`benchmark-results/mantle-splinterdb-20261013`: `inside7.sh`, `build-inner.sh`,
`splinter_bench.c`, image from `bench-image.Dockerfile`), or on macOS, where the harness needs Linux,
a runner in scratch with the same design and flags. Arms interleaved in a Williams order, each
case's rusage kept. All arms: 16-byte keys, 100-byte values, seed 301, a fill of NUM puts, 1M gets,
1M seeks of 10 rows, one client, no WAL, no compression, 64 MiB memtables.

- mantle: `benches/ranges.rs DB NUM 1 1000000 256 16 1000000 10 --rocks-seed=301 --shard-client
  --segments --machine-memory`: the product path, sized to the machine, each case's values checked
  against the fill's oracle and its reopened store verified key by key.
- `rocks`, `rocks16`, `rocks32`: `db_bench` 11.8.1 (`abeebd963`) with a 256 MiB, 16 GiB, 32 GiB block
  cache, `--max_background_jobs` at the cores. 16 GiB exceeds the 12-13 GB of rows at 100M; the
  32 GiB of research/58 and /66 would not fit these hosts.
- `sdb16all`: `splinter_bench` over SplinterDB `7d607b11`, 16 GiB cache, every other core in the
  background. SplinterDB's I/O is Linux-only (libaio), so macOS has no SplinterDB arm.

Every case that ran to its end exited 0, and its gets found the same count (632,048 at 10M, 631,247
at 100M), so the ratios compare the same answers. A busy host is the norm here and no case was
dropped for load. The cases not counted are named under each target. Each effect in the tables is
far past 5%, so those batches carry no A/A; the one effect under about 15% has one beside it (§ The
descriptors). `benches/shard_db.rs`, the engine alone, was built on macOS and not run.

**Why 100M falls short here.** A 100M get is fast when kept memtables and the cache answer it, and
this machine has too little room to keep them: a round kept 0-12 of 178 memtables after its fill, its
cache limit fell during the reads, and its queries went to the device (22,131 to 1,157,180 reads).
Compare the same binaries on 128 GB below: 178 of 178 kept, no device read. The compressor held
about 9 GB of other work during the sampling. The first suspicion, that `memory_available_now`
undercounts on macOS, did not survive measurement: sampled through a run, its reading was above an
estimate from file-backed pages, not below. It does count speculative pages twice
(`mach/vm_statistics.h`: they are "already accounted for in free_count"), a small overstatement left
as it is.

**Do not read the gets and seeks here as a lead.** The comparators' reads are 20-40 times below the
VM lane's at 100M, and they depend on the OS page cache, while this run's container was capped (32
GiB, then 48 GiB when the kernel's OOM killer twice ended the container during `sdb16all`; those two
cases are not counted and were run again) on a host short of memory and busy on its disk. The caps
are this run's own choice, so these ratios overstate the engine's lead by an amount not measured.
The fills, less dependent on the page cache, are the figures to read: below 4x at 100M.

The fd limit was raised to 1,048,576 for this batch, the limit Docker Desktop's containers have,
because main cannot run at this host's container default:

## The descriptors: main cannot fill at a 2,048 fd limit on 32 cores

Main failed all 9 of its cases at adacachy's container default (soft limit 2,048):
`open .../range-0.store.3: Too many open files`, at the third segment. `/proc/PID/fd` during a 3M
fill counted 576 handles of the base file and 1,443 of 11 segments. 576 = 32 maintenance workers x
(16 device workers + a reader), each its own `dup` from `issuer.rs`'s attachment, and each handle
opens each new segment for itself. So descriptors grow as workers x depth x segments, where
`segment_bound` in `benches/ranges.rs` allows `1 + depth + cores` a segment. It is independent of
architecture: the host's core count and limit decide. Docker Desktop's limit of about 1M hid it on
the VM lane. By the same arithmetic the M5 Max's shell limit of 10,240 would be passed at 100M, so
that run raised its limit and did not test this.

`2026-11-06-targets-hyper-block-fd.patch` shares one open file among a handle's duplicates, which is
what `dup` shares, each handle keeping its own flags and read window. It is not applied: `vendor/
hyper-block` is a snapshot of hyper-raft and "never edited by hand" (`scripts/snapshot-hyper-raft.py`),
so the change belongs in hyper-raft. Applied to a copy:

- hyper-block's `segmented` (7) and `file` (16) tests pass with one new test, on macOS only; the
  workspace gates were not run on it;
- a 100M case completed at the 2,048 limit, recovery verified, peak 1,085 + 32 store descriptors over
  39 segments;
- at 10M, 6 rounds on adacachy, three arms in a Williams order at a raised limit:

| | fills | gets | seeks |
|---|---|---|---|
| patched over main | 0.88x (0.73-1.05) | 1.04x (0.79-1.43) | 1.00x (0.81-1.13) |
| main over its own copy (A/A) | 1.03x (0.98-1.12) | 1.09x (0.68-1.82) | 1.05x (1.01-1.38) |

Fills about 12% slower sits outside this A/A's 3%, so the patch has a cost to explain or a measurement
to repeat before it lands; its cause was not traced. Gets and seeks cannot be told from the A/A.

## Open: wrong answers at 100M under `sample` on macOS

**Closed 2026-11-11:** a page cache took pages from a loan another cache had lent, when memory
pressure dropped and replaced the cache while a job held a loan; fixed and measured in
[2026-11-11-wrong-reads.md](2026-11-11-wrong-reads.md). What follows is the record as it stood.

Four 100M cases on the 36 GB Mac failed while Apple's `sample` was attached from the start of the
gets, on three builds, within about 50 minutes of 2026-11-06 (17:55-18:45 UTC).

| Build | Sampled cases | Failed | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| unmodified | 24 (1 more not counted) | 2 | `Corruption { "a branch page", UnknownTag(0) }`; `found flag differs from the fill oracle` |
| diagnostic 1 | 5 | 1 | `Corruption { "a branch page", UnknownTag(0) }` |
| diagnostic 2 | 6 | 1 | below; ended by `Corruption { "a branch page", Truncated }` in its seeks |
| diagnostic 3 | 24, 10 of them beside a 14 GiB memory hog | 0 | |

Twenty of the unmodified build's cases ran later as two loops overlapping by mistake, sharing case
directories, so they also competed for this Mac's memory; 19 were clean. The twentieth failed with
`found flag differs` at 20:35 UTC and is **not counted**: about five minutes earlier its live store
was deleted as a leftover, by the operator of this run. So the failures do not reproduce on demand,
and why they gathered in that window is unexplained. No case failed without `sample`: 11 on this Mac
at 100M (3 of them under SIGSTOP/SIGCONT at about 50 Hz), 4 on the 128 GB Mac, 18 at 10M, and every
Linux case. That absence is weak evidence at this rate; whether `sample` causes the failure or only
widens a race is not known.

What diagnostic 2 recorded, in its one failing run:

- 1,840 acknowledged keys answered "not found", each the same on three immediate re-reads, clustered
  by key (14.7M, 32.2M, 73.1-73.2M, 74.2M, 76.1M), plus 33 get errors: wrong for whole pages, and
  persistent while the process ran;
- backtraces through `Trunk::get_hashed` to `branch::point::read_value` on the shard's thread;
- the pages decoded as branch leaves held other things: 8 sightings began `mantleTK`, a trunk image's
  header, and most of the rest runs of consecutive little-endian integers. A 3,972-byte payload.

A device read verifies its checksum and address before decoding (`store/page.rs`), and a cache hit is
not verified again, so a page of another kind reaching the decoder as a live leaf points at either
the cache's view of an address (a slot, a loan adopted late, an extent's pages not forgotten before
its reuse) or two owners of one extent. Diagnostic 3 compares the page against a direct read of the
file on the first wrong answer and carries the run through to its reopen check, which would tell these
apart and say whether anything durable is wrong; it never failed, so this is unanswered. Whether acknowledged data was lost on disk is **not known**:
the failing runs ended before their recovery check.

A wrong "not found" passes no checksum and is caught here only by the bench's oracle, so this should
be treated as a correctness defect until shown otherwise.
