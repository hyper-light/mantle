# The engine on every supported target, beside the build of 2026-11-05

2026-11-19. mantle `29e42e9d` (B) beside `e118c09d` (A), the build `2026-11-06-targets.md` measured.
Hosted GitHub runners, the workflow `bench-targets` (run 44437096594), its bisect `bench-bisect`
(run 44439551008) and its syscall count `bench-syscalls` (run 44440095756).

**Question.** Does the engine hold its fills, gets and seeks on each supported target, Linux and
Windows on x86_64 and aarch64 and macOS on aarch64, against the build steps 1 and 2 were measured
at, Windows included, which `2026-11-06-targets.md` could not reach?

**Answer.** Gets and seeks hold on all five targets (medians 0.97x to 1.11x of A). The fill's own
rate holds on Linux (0.98x, 1.02x) and is 0.86x to 0.91x on macOS and Windows, past the range of
some rounds but not of others. The fill paid with its flush is 0.64x to 0.74x on Linux. That
difference is durability, not speed lost: A acknowledged a fill's runs before the segments other
maintenance workers wrote were flushed (hyper-block change 17, `vendor/hyper-block/ORIGIN.md`), so
its post-fill flush synced little and the kernel wrote the rest back later; B flushes every segment
a batch wrote before the batch is answered (CLAUDE.md §6). On one runner, interleaved: 23
`fdatasync` calls at 0.24 ms of CPU each before shared-fds, 36 to 37 at 5.5 to 17.7 ms after, the
post-fill flush 3.7 s and 7.2 to 7.4 s. No comparison here holds the two builds to the same
durability; one that does is the fill's own rate beside RocksDB's, which syncs its flushed files
too (`2026-11-06-targets.md`, How it ran).

## The post-fill flush on Linux

The fill's own rate (`fillrandom`) is the rate of its puts; the fill paid (`paid_fill`) adds the
flush that leaves its rows durable (`postfillflush`). On Linux that flush took twice as long at B.

Bisected on hosted Linux x86_64 runners, three runs at each point, a runner each (`bench-bisect`):
1.6-1.8 s at A; 2.5-5.5 s at three points to 2026-11-15; 1.2-1.4 s at `e6bf5002`, which merged
ci-health (`b543fb0c`: the device's issuer on Linux in the kernel, which the bench's pool issuer
does not use); 8.4-9.2 s at `b95e5ff4`, the next commit, which merged shared-fds (`2582d060`: one
open file a segment, its writes and flushes counted on the segment); 7.1-9.3 s at every later point
but one (4.2 s). The two adjacent points place the change in shared-fds.

Counted on one runner (`bench-syscalls`), the three builds in turn, under `strace -f -c` for the
calls and once more without it, twice, for the time:

| Build | post-fill flush | `pwrite64` | `fdatasync` | CPU in `fdatasync`, a call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `e6bf5002`, before shared-fds | 3.73 s, 3.66 s | 28,444 | 23 | 0.24 ms |
| `b95e5ff4`, shared-fds merged | 7.37 s, 7.31 s | 28,984 | 36 | 5.53 ms |
| `29e42e9d`, B | 7.19 s, 7.33 s | 26,175 | 37 | 17.72 ms |

The writes are the same; the flushes are 1.6 times as many, and each writes back twenty to seventy
times what one did (a flush writes the file's dirty pages in the caller, `filemap_write_and_wait`).
Before shared-fds each handle kept its own marks, and the device's issuer flushed a batch on one
worker's handle after its writes ran on any workers' handles, so a segment written only through
other workers' handles was not flushed with its batch (`vendor/hyper-block/ORIGIN.md` change 17):
those batches were answered with their bytes in the page cache. Change 17 counts writes on the
segment, so a batch's flush covers every segment written since its last, whoever wrote it. The
flush A did not pay is the durability a batch's answer promises.

What would make it cheaper without giving durability up is writeback begun as the runs are written,
so the flush at a batch's end waits on less, and is the engine's to measure (Lean doors: the
fill's tail, steps 4 and 9). The shared-fds comparison (`2026-11-14-shared-segment-fds.md`) counted
the fill's CPU a put, which the flush's waiting does not spend, and not its time.

## What holds

- Correctness on every target: CI's gates at `29e42e9d` (run 44437080511), the engine's suites
  among them, and every case here found the fill's rows, verified the reopened store key by key and
  exited 0.
- The engine's gets and seeks, and its fill's own rate on Linux, within the rounds' range of A.
- The fill paid on Linux is slower for durability A did not have; on macOS and Windows the fill's
  own rate is 0.86x to 0.91x, its rounds' range crossing 1.0x on all three targets.
